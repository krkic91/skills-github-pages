**Passage 1:** The Attentive Gaze

A young consultant, eager to impress, began his presentation with unwavering eye contact. His seasoned mentor gently reminded him, "In some cultures, eye contact is seen as too direct." The consultant nodded, realizing, "It’s important to be mindful of cultural differences." During the coffee break, a colleague suggested a different strategy. "Let’s find a culturally appropriate approach," she offered, demonstrating how to adapt to different cultures to avoid misinterpretation of even simple communication styles and cultural norms in business etiquette.

**Passage 2:** Winning the Team Over

The project lead needed to convince the team to adopt a new software. "Let me explain why this is beneficial," she started, clearly outlining the advantages. One team member raised a concern. "I understand your concerns, but..." the lead responded calmly, ready to handle objections with a strong argument. Her goal was to influence a decision and gain buy-in by highlighting benefits and addressing reservations directly.

## Production

**Language at Work:** IT Scenario

**Direction:** Act out the scenario. Focus on clear communication and applying the target vocabulary and language skills naturally within the given IT context. (3-5 minutes)

1. Title: Problems and Solutions: Software Bug Report

2. Objective: To practice reporting a technical problem and suggesting a basic solution.

3. Scenario: A user reports a minor software bug to an IT support team member.

### 4. Roles

- Team Member 1: End-User (describing a simple problem)

- Team Member 2: IT Support (acknowledging the problem, asking clarifying questions, suggesting a basic fix)

### SUGGESTED FLOW OF CONVERSATION

1. Reporting the Issue (End-User): Describes the software problem simply

2. Acknowledging and Clarifying (IT Support)

3. Suggesting a Solution (IT Support)

4. Outcome (Both)
