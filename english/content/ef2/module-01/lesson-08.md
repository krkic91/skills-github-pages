## Role-play

### Scenario: You are working on an international team with a new colleague from another

country. You need to discuss how to communicate effectively while respecting each other’s cultural differences.

**Student A:** You are from a culture that values direct communication and clear instructions. You prefer short emails and direct feedback.

**Student B:** You are from a culture that values indirect communication and polite language. You prefer building relationships before discussing work.

Greeting and Small Talk

**A:** Hi (Colleague's Name), nice to meet you. How is your day going?

**B:** Nice to meet you too! In my culture, we usually start meetings with a little small talk. How do you like to begin?

Discussing Communication Styles

**A:** In my culture, we prefer direct communication. If something needs to be changed, we say it clearly.

**B:** I understand that in your culture, it ’s common to be direct. In my culture, we usually give feedback in a softer way.

<!-- Source: F2_M01_L08_v0.2.pdf, page 2 -->

Handling Misunderstandings

**A:** Oh, I didn ’t mean to offend you. In my culture, this is normal.

**B:** No problem! Could you clarify that, please?

Finding a Solution

**A:** Let ’s find a way that works for both of us. Maybe we can combine direct feedback with polite expressions?

**B:** That sounds good! Could you explain how this is done in your workplace?

### Role-play Analysis

Answer the following questions:

1. What was the main cultural difference between the two colleagues?

2. How did Student A and Student B adapt to each other ’s communication style?

3. Which key phrases helped resolve misunderstandings?

4. What was the final solution they agreed on?

5. How would you handle a similar situation in your workplace?

### Discussion

How can understanding cultural differences in communication help improve teamwork and professional relationships in a global workplace?

## Production

### Scenario: Two colleagues from different cultural backgrounds are working together on

a project. One prefers direct communication, while the other prefers indirect communication. A misunderstanding occurs.

### Student A: Comes from a culture where direct feedback is normal. They say

“This report is unclear. You need to make it better.”

**Student B:** Comes from a culture where indirect feedback is preferred. They feel uncomfortable but want to respond professionally.

**A:** This report is unclear. You need to make it better.

**B:** Oh… I see. Could you clarify what needs improvement, please?

**A:** The data analysis section is too short. Could you add more details?

**B:** I understand that in your culture, it’s common to be direct. In my culture, we usually give feedback in a softer way.

**A:** I didn’t mean to offend you. In my culture, this is normal.

**B:** No problem! Let’s find a way that works for both of us.
