## Role-Play

### Scenario: Two coworkers, Tony and Jay, are having a conflict about a project deadline.

### The manager, Henry, intervenes to resolve the conflict.

**Scene 1:** Conflict

**Tony:** Jay, I don’t think I can meet this deadline. I have too much on my plate.

**Jay:** I understand your concern, but we need to meet this deadline. The client is expecting it.

**Scene 2:** Intervention

**Henry:** Hey, what’s going on here? I can see that you’re both stressed.

**Tony:** Jay is pushing for a deadline that’s unrealistic.

**Jay:** Tony is not taking this project seriously.

**Scene 3:** Resolution

**Henry:** Okay, let’s find a solution. Tony, what do you need to meet this deadline?

**Tony:** I need an extra day or two to ensure the quality of work.

**Jay:** That’s doable. I can talk to the client and see if we can push the deadline back.

**Henry:** Great. Let’s work together to resolve this.

### Role-Play Analysis

Answer the following questions:

1. What did Henry do well in handling the conflict?

2. What could Jay have done differently?

3. How did Tony and Jay contribute to the resolution of the conflict?

4. What can we learn from this scenario about handling workplace conflict?

## Useful Phrases in Handling Workplace Issues

1. Let’s work together to resolve this. – used in conflict resolution, problem-solving, improving communication, or building trust to foster a culture of collaboration, creativity, and mutual support.

### Example

- I understand we’re facing a challenge. Let’s work together to resolve this and find a solution that benefits everyone.

2. I understand your concern. – used to acknowledge and validate emotions, diffuse tension, create a safe space for discussion, and show willingness to listen.

### Example

- I understand your concern about the new policy, and I appreciate sharing your thoughts with me.

3. Let’s find a solution. – used in problem-solving, conflict resolution, improving processes, and/or enhancing employee engagement.

### Example

- Let’s find a solution that works for everyone. Any idea?

4. I see your point, but… – used to acknowledge the other person’s perspective while also introducing an alternative viewpoint or concern.

### Example

- I see your point about the new policy, but it’s essential for maintaining compliance with the industry regulations.

5. How can we resolve this? – used in conflict resolution, problem-solving, improving processes, and/or addressing customer complaints.

### Example

- We are experiencing a problem with our supply chain. How can we resolve this and minimize the impact on our customers?

skills in handling workplace issues.

## Production

Be more confident in handling workplace issues by answering the following questions.

1. How can we address the employees’ concerns in a fair and timely manner?

2. In communication issues, how can we ensure that all parties are on the same page?

3. What are the specific performance issues that need to be addressed?

4. How can we maintain a positive and productive working relationship?

5. How can we prevent similar conflicts from arising in the future?

## Wrap-Up

Conflict can be sensitive, often involving strong emotions. Allowing those involved to take ownership and collaborate on a solution can lead to lasting results. Ask open-ended questions about what happened, avoiding accusations or commands, and encourage them to suggest a course of action.
