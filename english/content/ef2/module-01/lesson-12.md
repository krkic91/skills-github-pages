## Warm-Up

Think about your current or previous work experience.

1. How do you usually update your manager or team about your tasks?

2. What details do you include?

## Role-Play

### Scenario: Sophia, a project manager, asks Tom, a software developer, for an update on

his current task.

**Sophia:** Hi David, can you give me a quick update on the bug fix for the payment system?

**Tom:** Sure! I’ve identified the issue—it was caused by a recent API change. I’ve already fixed the main problem and tested it in the staging environment.

**Sophia:** That’s great. When do you think it will be ready for deployment?

**Tom:** I just need to run one more round of testing. If everything looks good, I can deploy it by tomorrow afternoon.

**Sophia:** Sounds good! Keep me posted if anything changes.

**Tom:** Will do!

### Role-play Analysis

1. What task is David working on?

2. What was the cause of the issue?

3. What steps has David completed so far?

4. When does David expect to complete the task?

5. What does Emma ask David to do if there are any changes?

## Practice

A. Fill-in the blanks.

### Instructions: Read the conversation below and fill in the blanks with appropriate words.

Then, practice speaking the dialogue with a partner.

**Manager:** Hi ____________ (partner’s name), can you give me a quick update on your current task?

**Employee:** Sure! I am working on __________ (task name), and I have already __________ (completed step).

**Manager:** That’s good to hear. Have you faced any challenges?

**Employee:** Yes, I had an issue with __________ (problem), but I ____ (solution taken).

**Manager:** Great! When do you expect to finish?

**Employee:** If everything goes well, I should be done by __________ (timeframe).

**Manager:** Sounds good! Keep me __________ if anything changes.

**Employee:** Will do!

B. Reporting Task Progress

### Instructions: Read each question, choose the best answer, and explain your choice in

a full sentence. Then, practice saying it out loud.

1. What is the best way to start a task update?

a) I don’t know what’s happening.

b) I have some updates on my task.

c) Why do you need an update?

The best way to start a task update is _________________.

2. How should you describe progress on a task?

a) I finished everything, but I forgot the details.

b) I have completed the first two steps and will start testing tomorrow.

c) It's complicated, so I won’t explain.

To describe progress, I should say __________.

3. If there is a delay, how should you explain it?

a) There is a small delay because we found a bug, but we are fixing it now.

b) I don’t know when it will be done.

c) It’s late, but I don’t want to talk about it.

If there is a delay, I should say __________.

4. How can you politely ask for more time to complete a task?

a) I need more time. Just wait.

b) I underestimated the time needed, but I should be done by tomorrow.

c) I won’t be able to finish, and I don’t know when it will be ready.

To ask for more time politely, I should say __________.

## Key Vocabulary

| Word/Phrase | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I’d like to give you a<br>quick update on (task<br>name). | To introduce your update<br>clearly and professionally. | I’d like to give you a quick<br>update on the software bug<br>fix. So far, I have identified<br>the issue and tested a<br>solution |
| I have finished<br>(task/stage) | To explain what has been<br>done so far. | I have finished the initial<br>testing, and the task is 80%<br>complete. I expect to finish by<br>Friday. |
| There was a delay due<br>to (reason), but I’m<br>working on it now. | To explain difficulties and<br>how you are handling<br>them. | There was a delay due to an<br>API error, but I’m working on<br>a fix now. |
| Estimate | A rough time or number for<br>completion | I estimate that the task will<br>take two more days. |
| Ahead of schedule | The task will be completed<br>earlier than expected. | We are ahead of schedule<br>and should finish by<br>tomorrow. |
