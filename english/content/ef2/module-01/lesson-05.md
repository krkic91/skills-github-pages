## Grammar

1. Simple Present Tense

- Use: Describe facts, routines, and processes

###  Examples

- [x] The server updates its software automatically every night.

2. Passive Voice

- Use: Emphasize processes and results instead of the performer.

- Structure: [Object] + is/ are + past participle

###  Examples

- [x] The developer writes the code for the new application.

- [x] The system administrator monitors the servers’ performance continuously.

3. Relative Clauses for Describing Roles and Departments

- Use: Provide extra details about IT roles, processes, or systems

###  Examples

- [x] The system architect, who designs the network infrastructure, ensures that the data flow is secure and efficient.

- [x] The testing department, which validates the software’s functionality, identifies and reports any bugs before the release.

<!-- Source: F2_M01_L05_v0.2.pdf, page 2 -->

Use who (for people), that/which (for things), and commas to add other details.

4. Complex Sentences for Professionalism

- Use: connect multiple ideas logically in emails, reports, and presentations

###  Examples

- [x] Although the project manager coordinates with various departments, such as

Software Testing and Network Engineering, the reporting structure dictates that all technical issues must first be escalated to the Department Head, who then assigns resources accordingly.

5. Conjunctions (e.g., because, although, since) improve clarity in explanations.

## Practice

1. Role-Play: Quick Networking

Work in pairs and role-play 1-2 business scenarios using relevant key phrases. You can repeat with a new partner and new card or switch role after each round.

### Useful phrases

- “I’m responsible for…”

- “I’d like to introduce…”

- “Nice to meet you. What do you do at [company]?”

- “Where are you based?”

- “How’s your current project going?”

### Scenarios

1. You're meeting a new client at a conference. Introduce yourself and ask a question about their company.

2. A new colleague joins your team. Greet them, introduce your role, and ask about their experience.

3. You’re starting a meeting. Introduce yourself and give a quick overview of the agenda.

<!-- Source: F2_M01_L05_v0.2.pdf, page 3 -->

4. You’re at a networking lunch. Ask polite small talk questions about location, travel, or recent events.

5. You’ve just been introduced to someone from another department during a company event. Ask them about their role, team, and current project.

2. Mini Debate – “Agree or Disagree?”

Work in pairs or small groups and choose 1-2 business opinions below. Decide if you agree or disagree, and explain why. Use examples from your experience.

### Statements

1. “Introductions in meetings should always be short.”

2. “Networking online is more effective than networking face-to-face.”

3. “Organizational charts are not important; people just work together anyway.”

4. “Small talk wastes time in business meetings.”

5. “In international teams, it’s better to avoid personal topics.”

### Useful language

- “I agree because…” / “I disagree because…”

- “That’s an interesting point, but I think…”

- “Can you give an example?”

- “In my experience…”

- “What do you think about…?”

## Production

### ROLE-PLAY: BUSINESS SCENARIOS

Pair up and act out the assigned scenarios. Add more to the dialogue to keep the conversation going for 2 minutes using the appropriate phrases or vocabulary.

1. Professional Introductions

- (Person A): "Hello! It's quite a gathering. What brings you here today?"

- (Person B): "Nice to meet you. I’m representing the marketing department. And you?"

- (Person A): "Ah, interesting! I work in product development at Innovate

Solutions. Let me introduce myself properly..."

- (Person B): "It's great to connect with someone outside my usual team. Would you be open to exchanging contact details?"

2. Building Business Relationships

- Person A: "Interesting presentation on supply chain solutions."

- Person B: "Thanks! We're always looking for improvements."

- Person A: "I saw some potential opportunities there."

- Person B: "Oh! Tell me more."

3. Explaining Organizational Structures

- "Our company is structured into multiple departments, each with its own specific focus."

- "I personally report to the Head of Marketing."

- "The marketing team is responsible for developing and executing our promotional strategies."

- "How are things organized in your company?"
