## Role-Play

Read the conversation between Mike and Meg.

**Mike:** Hi Meg, do you have a minute? I’m still trying to get my head around the company structure here.

**Meg:** Sure, Mike. It can be a bit complex at first. What are you finding confusing?

**Mike:** Well, I understand we’re a subsidiary of a larger international group, but how does that affect our day-to-day work?

**Meg:** Right. So, we have a lot of autonomy, but certain decisions go to the corporate level. For example, major financial approvals.

**Mike:** Okay, and what about the different divisions? I’m in Marketing, but I’m not sure how we connect with Sales.

**Meg:** We work closely with them. Each division has a Head of department, and they coordinate regularly. Our reporting structure is quite clear. You report to your Team

Leader, then they report to the Head of Marketing, and so on.

**Mike:** That makes sense. Speaking of Team Leaders, I like mine. They are very supportive. What about the overall company culture here?

**Meg:** It’s pretty good. We try to foster a collaborative environment. Everyone is encouraged to share ideas, and there are regular team-building events.

**Mike:** That’s great to hear. I was a little worried about the size of the company, but it seems well-organized.

**Meg:** Yes, it is. The clear reporting structure and defined divisions help everyone understand their roles and responsibilities.

**Mike:** Thanks, Meg. This has really cleared things up.

**Meg:** No problem, Mike. If you have any other questions, just ask.

## Grammar

1. Simple Present Tense

**Use:** Describe facts, routines, and processes

**Example:** “Our company has five divisions: Sales, IT, HR, marketing, and Finance.”

2. Passive Voice

**Use:** Emphasize processes and results instead of the performer.

**Structure:** [Object]+is/ re + past participle

**Example:** “The company is structured into different departments.”

3. Relative Clauses for Describing Roles and Departments

**Use:** Provide extra details about IT roles, processes, or systems

**Example:** “The security update, which was released last week, fixed major bugs.”

4. Complex Sentences for Professionalism

**Use:** connect multiple ideas logically in emails, reports, and presentations

**Example:** “Although the new system is faster, some employees prefer the old one.”

5. Conjunctions (e.g., because, although, since): improve clarity in explanations.

## Summary

 Companies have structured hierarchies with key roles such as CEO, managers,

Team Leaders, and Departments.  Each level has distinct responsibilities that contribute to business operations and efficiency.  Practice explaining a company’s hierarchy in discussions and presentations to improve fluency and confidence.  A well-structured explanation of a company’s organization builds credibility and professionalism.
