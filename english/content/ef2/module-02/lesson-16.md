## Role-Play

### Scenario: David is working on a project with Pia, and he needs some assistance. Pia is

busy with her own tasks, but you’d like to ask for their help.

**David:** Hi, Pia. How does your schedule look today?

**Pia:** Well, I am reviewing my report before I submit it. Why?

**David:** I’m working on the project report and I’m having some trouble with data analysis. Would you mind helping me out?

**Pia:** Let me know how I can help. What specifically do you need assistance with?

**David:** I’m having trouble understanding the trends in data. I’d really appreciate it if you could look and offer some insights.

**Pia:** Okay, I can take a look. If it’s not too much trouble, can you help me with my task? I need someone to review my report before submission.

**David:** Absolutely, I’d be happy to help. Let’s trade favors – you help me with your data analysis, and I’ll review your report.

## Making Requests & Offering Help: Phrases

1. Could you please + Base Verb

### Example

- Could you please help?

- Could you please send it?

2. Would you mind + V_ing

### Example

- Would you mind helping me?

- Would you mind calling the client?

3. Would it be possible to… – used to make a polite request and to show consideration for the other’s time and availability.

### Example

- Would it be possible to get your feedback on this report?

- Would it be possible for you to help me with this task?

4. Let me know if you need any help. – used to offer assistance or support.

**Example:** Let me know if you need any help with this project.

5. If it’s not too much trouble… – used to acknowledge the other person’s time and effort.

**Example:** Would you mind reviewing this document if it’s not too much trouble?

<!-- Source: F2_M02_L16.pdf, page 3 -->

6. I’d really appreciate it if… – used to show gratitude and appreciation in advance.

**Example:** I’d really appreciate it if you could review this document before I submit it.

7. Let me know how I can help. – used to help, support, and willingness to help.

**Example:** Let me know how I can help you with this project.

## Production

### ROLE-PLAY: MAKING REQUEST AND OFFERING HELP

- Work in pairs.

- Each pair will be provided with one of the role-play scenarios below.

- One member will take on the role of the person making a request, while the other will be the person offering help.

- Act out the role-play using polite phrases from the vocabulary.

- If time permits, switch roles and practice with another scenario.

<!-- Source: F2_M02_L16.pdf, page 5 -->

### SCENARIO

**Scenario 1:** The Report Deadline

- Requester: You need to finish a project report by the end of the day but are struggling with some sections.

- Helper: You have already completed your own report and can offer help with proofreading and giving feedback.

**Scenario 2:** Moving Day

- Requester: You are moving to a new apartment this weekend and need help with lifting heavy boxes.

- Helper: You’re willing to help with heavy lifting, but you also have some errands to run that day. Offer to help if you can work out a time.

**Scenario 3:** Preparing for a Presentation

- Requester: You’re preparing for a big presentation and need assistance with practicing your delivery.

- Helper: You have experience with presentations and can offer to listen and provide constructive feedback, but you need to finish a project first.

**Scenario 4:** Running Errands

- Requester: You need to pick up medication from the pharmacy and also need to go grocery shopping but are short on time.

- Helper: You can offer to pick up the medication for Jamie while you’re out, but ask if Jamie can handle the grocery shopping.

**Scenario 5:** Team Project

- Requester: You are struggling with certain aspects of a team project and need help from a teammate.

- Helper: You’re available to help and suggest meeting after class to discuss the project in detail.
