## Role-Play

### Scenario: David is asking for everyone’s ideas during the meeting.

**David:** Alright, everyone. Does anyone have any ideas regarding our new project concept?

**Pia:** We need to create something that will link our future products together and can be controlled via smartphone.

**David:** That’s an interesting point, Pia. What do you think, Daniel?

**Daniel:** Well, we don’t currently have anything like that, and I think there’s a lot of potential there. Pia, could you further clarify the information needed on that?

**Pia:** We can use the information gathered for data analysis and statistics.

**David:** Karla, do you have anything to add?

**Karla:** That’s a great point, Pia. I think it could be extremely useful for us and for our customers. I’d like to add that we must create an entirely new system to manage the data.

**David:** Thank you for your input, Karla. Does anyone else have any additional ideas?

### Role-Play Analysis

Answer the following questions:

1. What did you learn from this role-play?

2. How did you feel about participating in the discussion?

3. What would you do differently in a real meeting?

## Participating in a Meeting Useful Phrases

1. I’d like to add something – used to contribute additional information or insights to the discussion.

### Example

- I’d like to add something to that point.

2. Could you clarify that? – used to seek additional information or explanation on a topic.

### Example

- Just to make sure I’m clear, could you clarify that point further?

3. That’s an interesting point / That’s a great point – used to acknowledge and show appreciation for someone’s idea or comment.

### Example

- That’s an interesting point, thank you for sharing!

- That’s a great point; can we explore it further?

4. Can I have a word here? / May I jump in? – used to politely interrupt and contribute to the discussion.

### Example

- Excuse me, can I have a word here?

- Excuse me, may I jump in?
