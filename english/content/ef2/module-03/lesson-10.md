## Practice

Group Discussion

### Work in pairs or small groups to discuss and review the following topics

1. Workplace Communication: Reflect on a cross-functional communication breakdown. How would you resolve it using active listening and clarity strategies?

- What caused the communication breakdown in this situation?

- What specific actions could each team member take to improve clarity?

- How can active listening prevent misunderstandings in cross-functional teams?

<!-- Source: F2_M03_L10.pdf, page 2 -->

2. Cross-Cultural Meeting: You’re in a cross-cultural meeting with misunderstandings between team members. How would you use cultural understanding to find common ground?

- What misunderstandings might arise in a cross-cultural meeting, such as differences in politeness, directness, or priorities?

- What are some diplomatic phrases or strategies you could use to remain neutral? (e.g. "From my cultural perspective..."; "Let’s find common ground.")

- How would you show cultural empathy to acknowledge diverse team members' viewpoints?

3. Chairing a Meeting: As a meeting chairperson, what techniques would you use to keep discussions structured, inclusive, and on time?

- How would you open the meeting and explain the agenda clearly?

- What would you do if one person dominated the conversation? How could you invite quieter participants to share their thoughts?

- What would you do to reinforce time management during the meeting?

## Production

### ROLE-PLAY: CHAIR A MEETING

**Context:** Your team is tasked with deciding how to allocate a $100,000 budget for

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) initiatives. The goal is to reach a consensus

### on how to divide the funds across three priorities

- Environmental sustainability (e.g., reducing carbon footprint).

- Community education programs (e.g., skills training for local youth).

- Disaster relief efforts (e.g., aid for natural disasters).

### Step 1: Choose a Role

Before the meeting, review the descriptions and phrases for the host styles. Prepare to adapt your communication style to match the role you’re assigned.

<!-- Source: F2_M03_L10.pdf, page 3 -->

|   | Approach | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host A | - Direct, task-focused, and goal-driven.<br>- Prioritize efficiency and clear decision-<br>making | - “Let’s dive right in. What’s<br>your top priority, and<br>why?”<br>- “Great point—let’s build on<br>that to move forward.” |
| Host B | - Polite, reserved, and consensus-driven.<br>- Avoid direct confrontation and prioritize<br>maintaining harmony.<br>- Express disagreement subtly and<br>consider long-term implications. | - “Would anyone else like to<br>share their thoughts<br>before we move forward?” |
| Host C | - Relationship-oriented and mindful of<br>hierarchy.<br>- Balance politeness with firmness and<br>prefer collaborative communication that<br>respects group harmony. | - “I appreciate all of your<br>perspectives. Let’s<br>evaluate the long-term<br>impacts of each idea.” |

### Step 2: Chair the Meeting

Work in small groups. Each person will take on one host role (A, B or C) and take turns chairing the meeting. While hosting, use cultural communication strategies appropriate for your role (e.g., direct vs. reserved, or relationship vs. task-focused).

### Step 3: Conclude the Meeting

Once everyone has had a turn hosting, work together to finalize how the $100,000 budget will be divided. Make sure all voices are heard, and the decision reflects effective collaboration.

### Discussion

- What were the differences in communication styles between each host?

- Which host approach was easier to adapt to? Which was more challenging?

Why?

- Did cultural differences influence the group dynamics or decision-making? How?

- What strategies helped the most in reaching a collaborative decision?
