## Case-study

Study the case-study below and answer the questions

### Case Study: Feedback on Performance

### Background

The marketing team at XYZ Corp has recently launched a new campaign. While the campaign received good initial traction, the overall results were below expectations. The team manager, Sarah, needs to have a difficult conversation with one of her team members, Tom, who was responsible for a significant portion of the campaign's content creation.

### Situation

Tom has been a valuable member of the team for the past two years, but Sarah has noticed that his recent work lacks the creativity and attention to detail that he previously demonstrated. In addition, Tom has been showing signs of disengagement during team meetings and often appears distracted.

### Objectives of the Conversation

- Sarah wants to provide constructive feedback to Tom about his recent performance without demotivating him.

<!-- Source: F2_M03_L16.pdf, page 2 -->

- She needs to understand Tom's perspective and any underlying issues that might be affecting his work.

- Sarah hopes to encourage Tom to regain his enthusiasm for the project and to discuss possible solutions to improve his performance.

### Case-study Analysis

1. How should Sarah prepare for the conversation? What points should she consider to ensure the meeting is constructive?

2. How can Sarah ensure that Tom feels comfortable during this difficult conversation?

3. What questions should Sarah ask to understand Tom’s situation better? What specific feedback should Sarah provide, and how should she deliver it?

4. How can they collaboratively create an action plan for improvement?

## Practice

A. Role-play Conversation

### Instructions: Practice role-playing the given scenario. To practice effective delegation

skills, take note of the highlighted phrases used in this dialogue.

**Situation:** Jamie has missed two important deadlines. Alex needs to address this issue without making Jamie feel attacked.

**Alex:** Hi Jamie, do you have a moment to talk?

**Jamie:** Sure, what’s up?

**Alex:** Thanks. I’d like to share some feedback about the last couple of projects.

**Jamie:** Okay, I’m listening.

**Alex:** I’m a bit concerned about the missed deadlines. I noticed the last two tasks were submitted late, and it affected the rest of the team.

**Jamie:** Yes, I know. I’ve been under a lot of pressure lately.

**Alex:** I understand this might be frustrating for you. Let’s try to stay focused on solutions. Could we find a way to help you manage your workload better?

**Jamie:** That would be helpful. Maybe I could work with someone on the next task?

<!-- Source: F2_M03_L16.pdf, page 3 -->

**Alex:** That sounds reasonable. I see your point, and I appreciate your honesty. Let’s check in again next week to see how things are going.

**Jamie:** Thanks, Alex. I’ll do my best to improve.

### Role-play Analysis

1. Which phrases did Alex use to express concern politely?

2. How did Alex acknowledge Jamie’s feelings?

3. Do you think the conversation was successful? Why or why not?

4. What other phrases could Alex have used to stay calm or supportive?

### Discussion

- Have you ever been in a situation like Alex or Jamie? How did you handle it?

Would you do anything differently now?

- Why is it important to stay calm and polite during difficult conversations at work?

What can happen if we don’t?

Example Conversation

**Sarah:** Hi Mark, can I talk to you for a minute?

**Mark:** Of course, what’s up?

**Sarah:** I’d like to share some feedback. In meetings, you sometimes interrupt others.

**Mark:** Oh, I didn’t realize that. I just get excited about ideas.

**Sarah:** I understand that. Let’s try to focus on giving everyone a chance to speak.

**Mark:** Got it. I’ll be more careful next time.

### Discussion
