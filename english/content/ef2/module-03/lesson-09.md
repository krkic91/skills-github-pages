## Case Study

The I.T. Department held a critical meeting to discuss the upcoming software deployment. However, the meeting quickly became an example of poor management, highlighting several key issues that hindered its effectiveness.

The meeting was scheduled on short notice, and many team members were not adequately prepared. The agenda was not shared in advance, leaving participants unsure of the topics to be discussed. As a result, the meeting started with confusion, and valuable time was spent clarifying the purpose and scope of the discussion.

Another issue observed during the meeting was that it was intended to last one hour, but it extended well beyond the allotted time. The facilitator failed to manage the discussion effectively, allowing certain topics to dominate the conversation while others were neglected.

Communication during the meeting was another major issue. Some team members dominated the conversation, while others struggled to voice their opinions. The lack of a structured approach to ensure equal participation resulted in important perspectives being overlooked.

## Practice

**Activity 1:** Role-Play

Practice role-playing the given scenario. Take note of the highlighted words and phrases used in this dialogue.

### Scenario: A manager is wrapping up a monthly meeting he has just finished for his

team. He was providing feedback for the supervisor who is going to conduct his first meeting next month.

**Manager:** Hi, Alex! I wanted to share some tips on running effective meetings.

**Alex:** Sure, go ahead!

**Manager:** First, great job on your recent work. Your efforts really stand out.

**Alex:** Thanks!

**Manager:** To manage noise, remind everyone to speak one at a time. It keeps things focused.

**Alex:** That’s helpful.

**Manager:** Engage everyone by asking questions. It makes the meeting lively and inclusive.

**Alex:** True, it does.

**Manager:** Finally, ask for feedback at the end. It helps us improve.

**Alex:** Good idea. Maybe we could add quick polls or brainstorming.

**Manager:** Great suggestion, Alex! Let’s try that next time.

**Alex:** Sounds good!

## Production

### CASE STUDY: CHAIR A MEETING

Work in pairs or small groups, reach each scenario and offer solutions to three common issues during disorganized meeting setups.

### Scenario 1

During your presentation in a monthly meeting, you noticed that one member sitting behind the table is constantly checking his mobile phone every minute, making you get distracted by the sound his phone creates.

### Analysis Questions

- How would you catch his attention and bring him back to the focus of your presentation?

- What strategies would you use to ensure that he stops and gets his focus back to the meeting table?

### Scenario 2

During the feedback session of your monthly meeting, you noticed that one staff member felt offended by your suggestion and was very vocal to disagree with you, creating an intense argument between another member.

### Analysis Questions

- How would you catch his attention and pacify this heated situation?

- What strategies would you use to make him realize the importance of giving feedback about his work progress?

### Discussion

1. Looking back at both scenarios, what personal strengths and weaknesses did you notice in your response strategies?

2. What would you do differently if you encountered these situations in real life?
